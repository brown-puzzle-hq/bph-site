This is a puzzle where you have to carefully follow a precise set of instructions to execute a tea party between six rabbits.

Solvers were given six rabbits of different colors, each with a letter on their front and back, and a plastic tophat. Additionally, they received six printed-out sheets of instructions and told only to look at their own sheets.

Before we get started, a brief guide on **counting instructions**. The Brown rabbit is responsible for counting the actions that take place over the course of the tea party and categorizing them into **steps**. Specifically, they will count one **instruction** each time a rabbit follows one of the bullet-pointed instructions on their sheet (general guidelines do *not* count), subject to modifications (either by the Brown rabbit or via information contained in that instruction). Every five **instructions**, the Brown rabbit calls out a **New Step**, which is the trigger for The Hatter reading out the letter on the side of their body facing the center of the table and carrying out their **Hatter Instructions**. These count as instructions for the Brown rabbit. 

A brief word on The Hatter: each rabbit's sheet has instructions on the back, and solvers are instructed not to look at them until they become The Hatter. These instructions generally disrupt the flow of the tea party in some way, or introduce additional complications. The Hatter carries these out at the start of a New Step, *after* reading out loud the letter written on the side of their body facing the center of the table. 

The Copycat Hatter's job is also worth mentioning here; at the start of a New Step, after The Hatter has completed their actions, they will read off any letter written on the inner side of the rabbit sitting across from them.

As a general rule, at the start of a new step, The Hatter will go first, read their letter, do their Hatter instructions, then the Copycat Hatter will read a letter (if they see one), then other instructions will follow.

Headers indicate **Steps**, subheaders indicate **Instructions**, according to Brown's count. Some subheaders are used for events that don't count as instructions (like drinking tea) and some subheaders jump up in count (when Brown counts an instruction twice). The indented blocks provide justification; SI abbreviates Standard Instructions, and HI abbreviates Hatter Instructions.

Note that I'm only including instructions that take place here; there are lots of instructions that *might* trigger that I'm not justifying the non-triggering of, but in each of these cases you can compare the instruction statements with the state of the tea party at the time to figure out why it souldn't trigger.

Submitting it on the site yielded a partial answer confirmation; 
- In-person teams got  "Do what must be done." Applying these as Caesar shifts to INSIDEHATTER, they obtained ANSWSOFTENUP, since the answer is **SOFTENUP**
- Remote teams got "Do what you must." and two pictures; one of the White rabbit beheaded, and the other of a slip of paper they would have found inside. This message read "SHIFT +10, -9, -5, -5 , +15, +10, -2, +19, -15, -6, +16, -2". Applying these as Caesar shifts to INSIDEHATTER, they obtained SENDSOFTENUP, since the answer is **SOFTENUP**.
